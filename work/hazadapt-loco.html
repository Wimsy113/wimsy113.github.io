01Overview

Find it, understand it, know what to do

Safety information isn't particularly useful if people can't find it, understand it, or figure out what they're supposed to do with it.

LoCo is a HazAdapt feature designed to help people access locally relevant safety and preparedness information. My work focused on improving how that information was organized, presented, and accessed across different devices.

The challenge was to make the experience easier to navigate without losing the context that makes safety information useful.

02The Challenge

Clarity without more explaining

Local safety information can become complicated quickly. Different hazards require different guidance, and information needs to be relevant to the person accessing it.

The interface needed to communicate that information clearly without overwhelming users with unnecessary controls or competing visual elements.

There were also practical considerations around mobile access, multilingual content, and how the experience behaved when connectivity wasn't reliable.

Rather than adding more explanations to an already busy interface, I focused on making the information itself easier to navigate.

03Organizing the Experience

Structure people don't have to see

Part of the design work involved understanding how safety content was structured.

LoCo used relationships between hazards, preparedness stages, and content placement to organize information. That underlying structure needed to make sense without requiring users to understand how the system worked behind the scenes.

I worked on clarifying hierarchy, simplifying navigation, and reducing visual clutter so people could concentrate on the information relevant to them.

04Iteration and Refinement

Useful isn't the same as helpful

As the feature developed, some of the interface treatments were creating more visual noise than clarity.

I revisited how spacing, grouping, borders, and iconography communicated relationships between content and actions. The goal wasn't to strip away functionality, but to make the existing experience easier to follow.

This meant refining the visual hierarchy and reconsidering elements that looked useful but weren't necessarily helping people complete their tasks.

05Accessibility Across Devices and Languages

Access shouldn't depend on ideal conditions

LoCo needed to work for people accessing information under different circumstances, including on mobile devices and with unreliable connectivity.

The experience supported seven languages: English, Spanish, French, German, Russian, Chinese, and Japanese.

That introduced considerations around text expansion, readability, responsive layouts, and making sure navigation remained understandable regardless of the language selected.

The work also included mobile beta considerations and offline fallback behavior, with an emphasis on keeping safety information accessible when conditions weren't ideal.

06Final Design and Takeaways

Simplifying isn't the same as removing

The resulting design work focused on clearer information hierarchy, more intentional navigation, and a less cluttered experience.

One of the biggest lessons from LoCo was that simplifying an interface doesn't necessarily mean simplifying the information behind it. Sometimes the complexity is necessary. The job is making sure users don't have to navigate all of it just to find what they need.

For a safety-focused feature, that distinction matters.
